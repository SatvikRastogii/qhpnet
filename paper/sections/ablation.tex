\subsection{Ablation Study}
Table~\ref{tab:abl} removes one mechanism at a time, paired against the full protocol on the 30 test topologies. Every selection and clustering mechanism pays for itself on both channels; the channel-learning layer pays only where there is loss to learn.

\emph{Hippopotamus Optimisation} costs 103 rounds of FND under loss (85 ideal) and $5.4\times10^3$ node-rounds of AUC when replaced by the top-$k_h$ quorum candidates: a ranking cannot trade one head's member load against another's, the max-stress fitness can. \emph{Quorum sensing} adds 45--57 rounds over a pool of the most energetic nodes, because its refractory term and local normalisation stop the same well-placed nodes being drafted epoch after epoch. Without the \emph{direct regime}, members that are cheaper to serve straight to the sink pay a head's fusion and amplifier costs ($-56$ to $-58$ rounds).

\emph{Stress-aware join} has the largest and most variable effect. Under loss, nearest-head joining costs 328 rounds on average (median 96), and more than 450 rounds in 8 of the 30 topologies, where members attach over links whose losses and load drain one head well ahead of the rest. Without loss the cost falls to 42 rounds. Its AUC is slightly \emph{higher} ($+1.2$ and $+2.0\times10^3$): the max--min trade-off again.

\emph{The pheromone memory} is insurance. Without ETX the median cost is 9 rounds, but two topologies lose 317 and 388, so the mean ($-35$, $p_{Holm}=0.08$) is not significant while the AUC loss is ($p<0.001$). On the ideal channel removing it changes nothing ($+1.5$, n.s.). Learning link quality from ACKs alone is as good as knowing it: substituting the true per-link PER changes FND by $+1.9$ rounds (n.s.).

\emph{A negative result.} A Physarum-inspired, congestion-aware router~\cite{tero2007physarum}, which reinforces paths in proportion to their flow, loses 80--84 rounds against greedy per-hop routing on both channels. Reinforcing the busiest paths concentrates relay load on the very nodes the max-stress fitness protects, so the two layers work against each other.
