\section{Introduction}
%% ======================================================================
A sensor node's battery is rarely replaced and its radio dominates its energy use, so the way a network organises its transmissions decides how long it lives~\cite{heinzelman2002leachc}. In clustering, members send to a cluster head (CH), which fuses their data and makes the one expensive transmission to the base station (BS)~\cite{heinzelman2000leach}. Every clustering protocol makes three decisions, implicitly or explicitly: \emph{who may become a head} (candidacy), \emph{which candidates serve and who joins whom} (selection and clustering), and \emph{how heads reach the sink} (routing).

Our previous study rebuilt nine protocols in one paired simulation engine~\cite{sharma2026unified}, and three of its findings motivate this paper. (i) Among protocols that deliver every reading, the multi-objective NSGA-II selector~\cite{deb2002nsga2} had the latest first node death (FND); the reactive TEEN family lived longer only by suppressing about 89\% of the readings~\cite{manjeshwar2001teen}. (ii) Learned selectors concentrated the head duty on a few nodes (duty Gini up to 0.70), which is what killed them early. (iii) Near the sink, clustering costs more than it saves, because a member's reading pays radio electronics twice, once to send and once to receive. Our earlier HIECF framework~\cite{sharma2026hiecf} combined type-2 fuzzy filtering, Q-learning and multi-hop routing. However, it was evaluated against behavioural models of its baselines rather than implementations, so its margins could not be tested for significance.

This paper proposes \textbf{QHP-Net}, which gives each of the three decisions its own bio-inspired mechanism, each aimed at one of those measured failure modes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Candidacy by bacterial quorum sensing}~\cite{miller2001quorum}. A node secretes an autoinducer in proportion to its energy, damped for a while after it serves, and becomes a candidate when its secretion exceeds the concentration around it. Candidates are therefore healthy, rested and spread over the field, which addresses (ii).
\item \textbf{Selection and clustering by Hippopotamus Optimisation (HO)}~\cite{amiri2024hippo}, minimising the predicted \emph{bottleneck stress} $\max_i \text{drain}_i/E_i$ rather than total energy~\cite{chang2004maxlife}. Members join whichever head, \emph{or the sink directly}, minimises their stress contribution. This switches clustering off where it does not pay (iii).
\item \textbf{Routing with an ant-pheromone link memory}~\cite{dorigo2006aco}. The protocol is never told the link quality. Each ARQ attempt evaporates a link's trail and each acknowledgement deposits on it, so the trail converges to the delivery probability. That probability gives an ETX~\cite{decouto2003etx} for forwarding between heads.
\end{itemize}
We compare QHP-Net with eight protocols in one engine, HIECF among them, over 30 paired topologies on lossy and ideal channels. We also test a 3$\times$3 size sweep, heterogeneous energy and stricter control accounting. The analysis uses Holm-corrected paired $t$-tests with Wilcoxon confirmation and a seven-variant ablation. QHP-Net's parameters were fixed on separate tuning seeds before any test run.

\section{Recent Work}
%% ======================================================================
Swarm metaheuristics dominate recent CH selection. Examples include an improved Harris hawks optimiser with fuzzy relays (HHOCFR)~\cite{jing2024hhocfr} and a multi-objective grey wolf (MOGWO)~\cite{sennan2025mogwo}. BSBOC, a binary secretary-bird optimiser, is one of the few to report a Wilcoxon test~\cite{abdulkareem2025bsboc}. Hybrids combine bat and bee colonies~\cite{mohammed2026baabc}, extended with multi-agent RL routing~\cite{mohammed2026marl}, or quantum PSO with a dueling double DQN~\cite{liu2026qpsodrl}. Slime-mould ideas appear in CH selection~\cite{belloula2026smawsn} and routing~\cite{zhang2013pirp}, and HO has been applied to CH selection with a mobile sink~\cite{kaushal2025hoam}.

Table~\ref{tab:recent} shows two recurring gaps. Few works model link loss, so they miss the retransmission cost on long sink hops that our earlier study found decisive~\cite{sharma2026unified}. Few report significance tests. To our knowledge, from searches of Crossref, OpenAlex and Europe PMC, no WSN protocol combines quorum sensing for candidacy, a metaheuristic for selection and a learned pheromone link memory for routing.

\section{System Model}
%% ======================================================================
$N$ static nodes are placed uniformly in a $W\times W$ field with the BS outside it at $(W/2,1.5W)$; the headline case is $N{=}100$, $W{=}100$\,m, $E_0{=}1$\,J (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Every node senses one reading per round, carried in a $k{=}4000$-bit packet. Radio energy follows the first-order model~\cite{heinzelman2002leachc},
\begin{equation}
E_{tx}(k,d)=kE_{el}+\begin{cases}k\varepsilon_{fs}d^2, & d<d_0\\ k\varepsilon_{mp}d^4, & d\ge d_0\end{cases},\qquad E_{rx}(k)=kE_{el},
\label{eq:radio}
\end{equation}
with $d_0=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{fs}/\varepsilon_{mp}}=87.7$\,m and a fusion charge $E_{DA}$ per bit received. A head emits one fused packet regardless of how many it receives (ideal fusion). Packet loss comes from a link budget: log-distance path loss with exponent $n{=}3$ and a per-link shadowing term $X_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,4^2)$\,dB drawn once per topology~\cite{rappaport2002wireless}, non-coherent BFSK bit errors, and $\text{PER}_{ij}=1-(1-\text{BER}_{ij})^k$. Failed packets are retried up to twice, and every attempt pays full transmit and receive energy. Control packets are 200 bits. Distributed protocols pay advertisement, join and TDMA schedule; centralised ones pay the schedule and the sink's downlink, and every transmitting non-head receives a schedule each round. A protocol returns only heads and next hops. It cannot write energy, and energy conservation is asserted to $10^{-9}$\,J in every run.

\section{Proposed QHP-Net}
%% ======================================================================
QHP-Net re-clusters every five rounds, and immediately if a head dies (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). The sink runs layers 2 and 3 from the state that candidates report. Let $E_i$ be the residual energy, $r_i^h$ the last round node $i$ served as head, and $k_h=\max(1,\text{round}(p\,N_{alive}))$ the head count shared by all protocols.

\subsection{Layer 1: Quorum-Sensing Candidacy}
Every node secretes an autoinducer
\begin{equation}
a_i=\frac{E_i}{\bar E_0}\,(1-\phi_i),\qquad \phi_i=e^{-(r-r_i^h)/\tau},
\label{eq:secretion}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_i$ is a refractory term that silences a node that has just served ($\phi_i=0$ if it never has). Neighbours within the density radius $\rho$ exchange $a_i$ in one beacon. Each node computes, from the beacons it hears and nothing else, the kernel-weighted concentration and its quorum ratio,
\begin{equation}
C_i=\frac{\sum_j K_{ij}a_j}{\sum_j K_{ij}},\quad K_{ij}=e^{-d_{ij}^2/2\rho^2}\mathbb 1[d_{ij}<\rho],\quad q_i=\frac{a_i}{C_i}.
\label{eq:quorum}
\end{equation}
A node with $q_i\ge1$ out-secretes its neighbourhood and expresses the candidate phenotype. The pool is the top $n_c$ nodes by $q_i(1-\phi_i)$, with $n_c=\text{clip}(|\{q_i\ge1\}|,3k_h,8k_h)$. Because $q_i$ is \emph{relative}, every region contributes candidates even late in life. Beacons, sent every four epochs, are charged as control packets; between them the sink applies the refractory factor itself. As for every centralised protocol here, node state reaches the sink on data traffic~\cite{sharma2026unified}; QHP-Net additionally pays a 200-bit report per candidate, and Section~\ref{sec:robust} charges every protocol's uplink.

\subsection{Layer 2: Hippopotamus-Optimised Selection and Clustering}
\emph{Stress model.} For a head set $\mathcal H$, let $\text{ETX}_{ij}=1+\hat p_{ij}+\hat p_{ij}^2$ be the expected number of attempts (at most three) under the learned loss estimate $\hat p_{ij}$ (Layer 3), with $c_{ij}=\text{ETX}_{ij}E_{tx}(k,d_{ij})$ and $\hat c_{ij}=\text{ETX}_{ij}(E_{rx}(k)+kE_{DA})$. Each alive non-head node joins
\begin{equation}
h^\star(i)=\arg\min_{h\in\mathcal H\cup\{\text{BS}\}}\;\sigma_{ih},\quad \sigma_{ih}=\frac{c_{ih}}{E_i}+\frac{\hat c_{ih}}{E_h},\ \ \sigma_{i\,\text{BS}}=\frac{c_{i\,\text{BS}}}{E_i},
\label{eq:join}
\end{equation}
charging each joule to the battery that pays it. Clusters shrink around weak heads, and nodes whose sink hop is cheaper than a second reception transmit directly (the \emph{direct regime}). Summing transmissions and receptions gives the predicted per-round drain $D_i(\mathcal H)$, and the fitness is
\begin{equation}
F(\mathcal H)=\max_i\frac{D_i}{E_i}+\mu\,\frac{1}{N_{alive}}\sum_i\frac{D_i}{E_i},
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the reciprocal of the predicted time to the next death~\cite{chang2004maxlife}.

\emph{Search.} A herd of $P{=}20$ hippopotamuses encodes $k_h$ points in the field; each point decodes to the nearest unused candidate. HO~\cite{amiri2024hippo} runs three greedily accepted phases for $T{=}25$ iterations. In the river phase males move toward the dominant hippo, $X_i'=X_i+y_1(D_{hippo}-I_1X_i)$, and immatures toward or away from a random group mean. Predator defence applies a L\'evy step~\cite{mantegna1994levy}, $X_i'=RL\odot X_{pred}+\frac{b}{c-d\cos 2\pi g}\cdot\frac{1}{\delta_i}$. Escape is a shrinking local search, $X_i'=X_i+r(\frac{lb}{t}+s_1(\frac{ub}{t}-\frac{lb}{t}))$. The herd is seeded with the top-$k_h$ quorum candidates and the previous epoch's best.

\subsection{Layer 3: Pheromone-Learned Routing}
Each node keeps a pheromone $\tau_{ij}\in(0,1]$ per link, initialised to 1: the protocol is given no channel model, no path-loss parameters and no knowledge of whether the channel is lossy. After each data packet with $n$ attempts,
\begin{equation}
\tau_{ij}\leftarrow\tau_{ij}(1-\varrho)^{n}+\varrho\,\mathbb 1[\text{acked}],\qquad \hat p_{ij}=1-\tau_{ij},
\label{eq:pheromone}
\end{equation}
which is the ant-colony evaporate-and-deposit rule~\cite{dorigo2006aco} applied to delivery. Its fixed point is the per-attempt success probability, learned from acknowledgements the sender already receives. A head forwards to $\arg\min_{j}\sigma_{hj}$ over the BS and the heads strictly closer to it, so routes are acyclic. With ideal fusion every downstream head transmits anyway, so $\sigma_{hj}$ is the exact marginal cost. The same rule is used inside $F$, so the optimiser and the deployed routes agree.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:qhp} summarises one epoch. Its cost is $O(N\rho^2)$ for quorum sensing and $O(PT\,N k_h)$ for the search, vectorised at the sink.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{QHP-Net epoch (every 5 rounds or on head death)}
\label{alg:qhp}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require residual energies $E$, head history $r^h$, pheromone $\tau$
\State $\hat p\gets 1-\tau$;\ \ $\text{ETX}\gets 1+\hat p+\hat p^2$ \Comment{L3 memory}
\If{beacon epoch} each node broadcasts $a_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:secretion}); compute $q_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:quorum}) \EndIf
\State $\mathcal C\gets$ top-$n_c$ nodes by $q_i(1-\phi_i)$; candidates report state \Comment{L1}
\State initialise herd $X\in[0,W]^{P\times k_h\times2}$; seed with top-$k_h$ of $\mathcal C$ and last best
\For{$t=1\ldots T$} \Comment{L2: HO}
  \State river phase (males $\to D_{hippo}$; immatures $\to/\leftarrow MG$); greedy accept
  \State predator defence with L\'evy flight; greedy accept
  \State escape inside bounds $[lb/t,ub/t]$; greedy accept
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal H\gets$ decode$(X_{best})$; heads route to $\arg\min_j\sigma_{hj}$ \Comment{L3}
\State members join $h^\star(i)$ or the BS (Eq.~\ref{eq:join})
\For{each round of the epoch} transmit; update $\tau$ from ACKs (Eq.~\ref{eq:pheromone}) \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Design}
%% ======================================================================
\emph{Protocols.} Direct transmission, LEACH~\cite{heinzelman2000leach}, PEGASIS~\cite{lindsey2002pegasis}, TEEN~\cite{manjeshwar2001teen}, NSGA-II (population 40, 30 generations, three objectives)~\cite{deb2002nsga2}, an interval type-2 fuzzy selector with 27 rules and Karnik--Mendel reduction~\cite{karnik1999type2}, a $4{\to}16{\to}2$ DQN~\cite{mnih2015dqn} trained offline on 40 disjoint seeds and then frozen, HIECF~\cite{sharma2026hiecf} (type-2 fuzzy filter, tabular Q-learning, $d^2/E$ multi-hop routing), and QHP-Net. All share the engine and $k_h$. NSGA-II and QHP-Net re-optimise every 5 rounds or on a head death; LEACH, TEEN, Fuzzy-T2, DQN and HIECF re-cluster every round, as in~\cite{sharma2026unified}.

\emph{Pairing and seeds.} Seed $i$ fixes the positions, energies, shadowing matrix and readings, so all protocols at index $i$ face an identical world. Test seeds are $0$--$29$. QHP-Net's parameters ($\tau{=}20$, $\mu{=}0.5$, $\varrho{=}0.1$, $P{=}20$, $T{=}25$, beacon period 4) were chosen on seeds $500$--$505$ only. Every setting is run with packet loss and on an ideal channel.

\emph{Metrics.} FND, HND and LND (first, half and last death; censored at 7000), area under the alive curve (AUC), yield (readings delivered over taken) and energy per delivered reading.

\emph{Statistics.} For each metric we form 30 per-seed differences $\Delta_i=x_i^{\text{QHP}}-x_i^{\text{opp}}$ and apply a two-sided paired $t$-test. We report the mean difference, its 95\% $t$-interval, Cohen's $d_z=\bar\Delta/s_\Delta$~\cite{cohen1988power}, Holm-corrected $p$ across opponents~\cite{holm1979simple}, a Shapiro--Wilk test of normality on $\Delta$~\cite{shapiro1965analysis}, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual} as a distribution-free check.
